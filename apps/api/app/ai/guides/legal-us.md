# Legal guide, United States (draft summary)

Status: written by the MyBiz team from public official sources as a starting point. Not yet reviewed by a lawyer. US law differs by state; always check the source named in each section and send anything important to a real lawyer.

## Privacy: CCPA and CPRA in California

The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA), gives California residents rights to know what personal information a business collects, to delete it, to correct it, and to opt out of its sale or sharing. It applies to a for-profit business that does business in California and meets a threshold: annual gross revenue above a set amount (adjusted for inflation), or buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households, or earning 50 percent or more of revenue from selling or sharing personal information. A business must publish a privacy policy and a notice at collection. Source: California Attorney General, https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa

## Privacy: other states and children

Many other states have their own comprehensive privacy laws with different thresholds and rights. The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) restricts collecting personal information from children under 13. Check the specific state before answering. Source: FTC business guidance, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance

## Email marketing: CAN-SPAM

The CAN-SPAM Act applies to commercial email. The header and sender information must not be false or misleading; the subject line must not mislead; the message must be identified as an advertisement; it must include a valid physical postal address of the sender; it must give a clear way to opt out, and the business must honour an opt-out request within 10 business days. The sender stays responsible even when it hires another company to send. Each violating email can carry a civil penalty. Source: FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business

## Text messages and calls: TCPA

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act restricts marketing calls and texts. Marketing texts sent with an automatic system generally need the recipient's prior express written consent; the consent must be clear, and must not be a condition of buying. Honour requests to stop (such as STOP) promptly. Calling and texting hours are limited to between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time. Source: FCC, https://www.fcc.gov/general/telemarketing-and-robocalls

## Accessibility: ADA

Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act applies to places of public accommodation, and courts and the Department of Justice have treated websites and apps of such businesses as covered, although the law does not name a technical standard for private businesses. WCAG 2.1 level AA is the usual benchmark; the Department of Justice adopted it by rule for state and local government websites. Lawsuits over inaccessible websites are common. Source: US Department of Justice, https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/

## Advertising and reviews

The FTC Act forbids unfair or deceptive advertising. Claims must be truthful and supported; endorsements and reviews must disclose material connections (for example free products or payment); fake reviews are prohibited. Source: FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing

## What this guide does not cover

Employment law, contracts, intellectual property, securities and court procedure are not in this library. For these, say that the library has nothing, and prepare questions for a lawyer.
