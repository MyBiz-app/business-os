# Accounting and tax guide, United States (draft summary)

Status: written by the MyBiz team from public official sources as a starting point. Not yet reviewed by a CPA. Figures change every year: always check the current IRS page, and send anything important to a real CPA.

## Choosing a business structure

A sole proprietorship, a partnership, an LLC, an S corporation and a C corporation are taxed differently. A C corporation pays the federal corporate income tax, at a flat 21 percent rate. Owners of a sole proprietorship, a partnership and most LLCs report business income on their personal return. Source: IRS, https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed and Publication 334, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334

## Self-employment tax and estimated tax

A self-employed person pays self-employment tax for Social Security and Medicare (15.3 percent, on net earnings up to the Social Security wage base for the Social Security part). Income tax and self-employment tax are paid during the year in quarterly estimated payments (Form 1040-ES). Source: IRS Publication 334.

## Business expenses

An expense is deductible when it is ordinary and necessary for the business. Personal expenses are not deductible. Some expenses have special limits (for example meals, vehicles, home office). Keep receipts and records. Source: IRS Publication 535, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p535

## Employees: payroll taxes

An employer withholds federal income tax and the employee's share of Social Security and Medicare (FICA) from wages, pays the employer's share, deposits the taxes on schedule, files Form 941 every quarter, gives each employee a Form W-2 by January 31, and keeps Form W-4 from each employee. State and local payroll taxes also apply. Source: IRS Publication 15, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15

## Contractors and Form 1099

A worker's classification as an employee or an independent contractor depends on the real relationship, not on the label. Payments to independent contractors above a reporting threshold are reported on Form 1099-NEC, usually by January 31. The threshold changes: check the current figure. Source: IRS.

## Sales tax

There is no federal sales tax. States, and often cities and counties, set sales tax rates and decide which goods and services are taxable; for example the taxability of software and services differs from state to state. Since South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018), a seller can have to collect sales tax in a state where it has no physical presence once it passes the state's economic nexus threshold (a sales amount or number of transactions). A seller registers in the state, collects the tax, and files returns. Always check the specific state's tax authority. Source: the state department of revenue.

## Recordkeeping

Keep tax records for at least three years from filing in the ordinary case, and longer in some cases (for example employment tax records for four years). Source: IRS Publication 334.

## What this guide does not cover

International tax, estate and gift tax, and audits are not in this library. Say that the library has nothing, and prepare questions for a CPA.
